1 · One launch shape

Strongin-process

plan.ts 161–216, 221–429, 435–583, 588–655 · spawn.ts 48–140, 437–500 · jobs.ts 15–100 · index.ts 114, 2450–2532

Before — one launch, three declarations

Every launch field is declared three times and copied twice. The copies are not decisions.

After — one declaration, two edges

Identity and transport become edges on one shape; the field list lives in one place.

ProblemThe launch data is restated in three declarations, and two functions exist only to copy field names between them.

Interface todayplanFromInput(input, PlanContext) with a 28-field context, returning a 35-field JobPlan; fire(intent, SpawnContext) with a 34-field context, building a 52-field WorkerSpecInput; recordFromPlan() copying 28 fields into a 65-field JobRecord.
What it hidesThe precedence table: definition vs call vs environment vs fork mode, MCP trust, permission modes, memory tools, compact window, background policy. That part is genuinely deep and should stay.
Callers must knowWhich of the three shapes a given option belongs to; that adding one option means editing PlanAgent, JobPlan, WorkerSpecInput, JobRecord, planFromInput, recordFromPlan, the fire spread, and the worker's spec.x read. The entry also assembles 28 context fields by hand.
Deletion testDelete JobPlan and WorkerSpecInput and make fire + the worker consume the record's field names. The 28 copies in recordFromPlan and the 35 spreads in fire disappear; the precedence logic stays in one function. Names that leave the caller: recordFromPlan, JobPlan, WorkerSpecInput, PlanContext, and 15 Plan* type exports. Complexity concentrates in planFromInput rather than spreading across three shapes.
  • one declaration per launch field
  • one place to add an option
  • 28-field context → ~6 capabilities
  • 15 dead type exports deleted
  • typecheck catches every drift
No runtime behaviour changes: planFromInput keeps the precedence table; only the shape it returns and the mapping around it collapse. The 137 smoke checks exercise spawn, resume, and send through the entry.

2 · Give the tool surface a session

Strongin-process

tools.ts 37–76 (context), 85–706 (createTools) · index.ts 2537–2568 (wiring)

Before — 30 loose collaborators

Three public methods hide the handlers, but construction demands the whole entry.

After — one session port

One port hides identity, job reads, the roster, and delivery behind six capabilities.

ProblemThe tool surface is deep in behaviour but shallow in construction: 30 fields and two sub-contexts must be handed in before one handler can run.

Interface todayToolsContext with 30 readonly fields, createTools(ctx) returning {register, outputText, sendToRecord}. Registration adds subagent_spawn, subagent_list, subagent_output, subagent_cancel, subagent_resume, subagent_agents, ListAgents, SubagentHandback, subagent_send, pubsub_send, plus pubsub_fire, pubsub_list, pubsub_cancel aliases.
What it hidesThe bounded wait loop, transcript JSONL parsing, log slicing, handback signal construction, alias registration, and the shape of each tool's schema. None of that leaks today: the three methods are a good interface.
Callers must knowWhich collaborator each handler needs, that sessionId and workerSpec are late-bound functions, that safeDrain is the only drain the tools may call, and that scanOutput/reportText must both be supplied. The entry's wiring is a 31-line object literal.
Deletion testDelete createTools and the 622 lines reappear in the entry, spreading handler knowledge across every command and panel. It earns its keep. Deepen by narrowing the constructor: names that leave the entry's wiring are readJob, writeJob, readRecentJobs, refreshJobs, enqueueSignal, safeDrain, cancelJob, ledger, currentOwner, ownerId, rosterNames, namedJob, liveState, appendLive, listText, scanOutput, reportText, listAgentsVisible (18 of 30), replaced by one session.
  • context 30 → 6 ports
  • locality: ordering rules in session
  • tests build one fake session
  • handlers stay private

3 · One record lifecycle, embedded

Worth exploringbundle-literal constraint

index.ts 295–1437 (WORKER_SOURCE, 1,143 lines) · state.ts 23–78 · jobs.ts 119–163 · signals.ts 1–119

Before — two lifecycles

The one-file bundle makes the worker a string, so it grows its own copy of the lifecycle.

After — one embeddable source

The worker keeps its one-file constraint; it stops owning a second lifecycle.

ProblemThe terminal-state rule and the “record and signal carry the same facts” rule are implemented in the parent and again inside the worker string.

Interface todayNone. The worker is an opaque 1,143-line template string with a 52-field JSON spec in its environment. Its whole surface is the files it writes.
What it hidesProcess supervision, the ACP transport, fork stdio parsing, the live-message loop, permission handling, and the two content rules that are shared: state transitions and signal construction.
Callers must knowThat two implementations exist and must stay in sync; that the worker's writeRecord force-writes canceled while the parent uses canTransition; that settle must not emit a second signal after handbackAt. A change to one rule is a two-file change with no typecheck across the string.
Deletion testDelete the worker's readRecord, writeRecord, and settle copies. If the parent's rules can be embedded with .toString() the way signalEnvelope and scanOutput already are, complexity concentrates in one reference-free source and disappears from the worker. Names that leave the worker: readRecord, writeRecord, settle. Risk: the bodies must stay plain, reference-free JavaScript; only node test/e2e.mjs covers the real host, and it is not in CI.
  • one terminal rule
  • one settle constructor
  • no string drift
  • leverage: 2 adapters, 1 rule table
Does not contradict ADR-0002 or ADR-0003: the bundle stays one file, and the worker still receives source. It extends the existing .toString() seam. Deferred behind candidates 1–2 because its failure mode is only visible in e2e.

4 · Owner identity behind a module

Worth exploringin-process

index.ts 1757–1853 (owner), 2067–2093 (reconcile reads the token directly)

Before — five names in the entry

The token's lifecycle is an implicit precondition on every caller.

After — one identity port

One module owns token minting, transcript persistence, and the pending verdict.

ProblemIdentity is four entry-local functions and a mutable token; the rule that a verdict is pending until the token is read is invisible to callers.

Interface todayFour functions: saveOwner(token), ownerId(), currentOwner(), ownerVerdict(owner), plus the ownerToken variable that reconcileJobs compares directly.
What it hidesMinting, one-time transcript persistence, reading the current and 0.1.0 custom entry types, worker inheritance from the spec, and the orphan-vs-foreign rule.
Callers must knowCall currentOwner() (or ownerId()) before ownerVerdict(), or every signal looks pending. That a worker must not mint its own token. That reconcile compares the raw token, not the verdict.
Deletion testExtract createIdentity behind {current, verdict}. Names that leave index.ts: ownerToken, ownerSaved, saveOwner, ownerId, currentOwner, ownerVerdict (6). The ordering rule moves inside the module, where it is testable with a fake session. Small capability, very low risk.
  • locality: token rules in one place
  • pending becomes an invariant
  • tests use a fake session
  • 6 entry names deleted

5 · One roster module

Worth exploringin-process

index.ts 1964–2062 (live), 2020–2041 (rosterFor), 1985–2004 (names)

Before — five scans, one record set

The roster rules live in the caller, so each new reader re-derives them.

After — one roster

Names, lookup, control queue, and snapshot share one owner filter.

ProblemFive entry closures re-derive “which named jobs are mine” from the same record scan, and the worker branch is re-checked in each.

Interface todayownedNamedJobs(), rosterNames(), namedJob(name), appendLive(id, from, message), rosterFor(selfName). Each is called directly by tools, spawn, commands, or the panel.
What it hidesAlmost nothing: the owner filter and the worker-roster gate are visible in each body. This is the shallow one.
Callers must knowThat a worker must be gated by workerSpec.rosterNames; that namedJob prefers an alive record over a newer finished one; that rosterFor appends the parent's inherited view when nested. Three rules, three call sites.
Deletion testExtract createRoster. Names that leave index.ts: ownedNamedJobs, rosterNames, namedJob, appendLive, rosterFor (5). If the extraction needs more than four inputs, it is two modules (names vs control queue); it does not.
  • one owner filter
  • one live-vs-idle preference
  • 5 names deleted
  • testable with a record list

6 · Split claim from delivery

Worth exploringreject for now

delivery.ts 25–70 (DeliveryDeps), 86–504 (createDelivery)

Before — 25 deps, 11 methods

The module is deep in behaviour but its constructor and method list are both wide.

After — two seams, one invariant

Splitting is only safe if the claim cursor and settle stay in one owner.

ProblemEleven methods and 25 dependencies for one job: the module owns both the atomic claim and three periodic jobs.

Interface todayDelivery with drain, safeDrain, claimPending, settle, markDelivered, formatSignalMessage, countPending, countOrphaned, refreshJobs, sweepClaimed, sweepOrphaned.
What it hidesThe rename-as-claim protocol, the inbox cursor, duplicate-signal handling, ledger replay, quarantine, permission answering, and the jobs-cache signature. This is real depth; the method count is the friction.
Callers must knowThat settle is public because the onStop path claims and settles outside drain; that countPending claims then holds; that refreshJobs runs hooks before the status line. Three ordering contracts spread over eleven methods.
Deletion testDeleting createDelivery would re-spread the claim protocol into the entry and the onStop handler; it earns its keep. Deepening would split claim/settle/count from format/drain/refresh, but the atomic invariant spans both. Names that leave the caller: claimPending, settle, markDelivered, countPending, countOrphaned, sweepClaimed, sweepOrphaned (7) become one claim facade. Rejected for now: the risk is losing a signal, and smoke covers the happy path better than the races.
  • interface 11 → 6
  • deps 25 → 5 + rest
  • claim invariant named

8 · Trim the re-export surface

Speculativedeletion, not deepening

index.ts 140–276 · readers: test/smoke.mjs, test/e2e.mjs, test/harness.mjs, test/bundle.mjs

Before — 153 names, 3 readers

The bundle advertises a public surface only four files use.

After — export what is read

The deletion test says pass-through: removing it concentrates nothing.

Problem153 names are re-exported from the entry; four test files read 56, and 24 value exports have no reader at all.

Interface todayOne export … from block per module across index.ts:140–276: 153 unique names, 76 values and 77 types.
What it hidesNothing. It is a pass-through of each module's surface.
Callers must knowWhich name is public and which is internal. The host imports only the default export; tests import from the bundle. The re-export list is a de facto API with no version contract.
Deletion testDelete the 24 unread value exports (moveTaskSelection, taskPanelRows, tasksHintVisible, PERMISSION_MODE_LIST, canonicalPermissionMode, resolvePermissionMode, resolveTeamPolicy, reportText, gitStatusSnapshot, skillFileFor, listAgentsVisible, worktreeChanged, resolveAppendSystemPrompt, BACKGROUND_BUILTIN_TOOLS, EFFORT_LEVELS, SUBAGENT_TOOL_UNIVERSE, UNIVERSAL_TOOL_REMOVALS, toolNameOfSpecifier, AGENT_SCOPES, DESCRIPTION_LIMIT_TOKENS, parseCacheTtl, parseHooks, parseMcpServers, TEAM_TOOL_NAMES). Nothing reappears in a caller: no one crossed that seam. This is a trim, not a deepening.
  • 24 dead values deleted
  • a truthful public surface
  • no behaviour change
Worth doing as cleanup after the structural change, not as the architecture target: it removes a false interface but creates no new locality or leverage.

Considered, not proposed

Places that look like candidates by size but do not pass the deletion test.

definitions.tsKeep whole. 1,221 lines, but the interface is one registry plus a handful of parsers; the YAML parser, builtins, and scope scan are one coherent implementation. Splitting would move declarations without concentrating a rule. The one real leak it feeds — PlanAgent restating AgentDefinition — is candidate 1.
spawn.tsKeep the launcher, narrow the spec. A 34-field SpawnContext is wide, but it is the worker launcher's genuine dependency set. The duplicated 52-field WorkerSpecInput is candidate 1; do not split fire separately.
startup.tsKeep. Eight exported functions for one consumer look wide, but each is a pure reader over an injected fs and the bundle is one implementation. Deleting the module would spread five filesystem walks across the entry.
index.ts:1441–3057Not one target. The entry body holds owner (candidate 4), live (candidate 5), and pane (candidate 7) groups; the rest is flags, hooks, commands, and lifecycle wiring. Treat it as a composition root, not a module to deepen wholesale.

Top recommendation: One launch shape

Candidate 1 is the only target that deletes a type layer rather than narrowing one context. JobPlan (35 fields), WorkerSpecInput (52), and JobRecord (65) are three declarations of the same launch; recordFromPlan and 35 spreads in fire exist only to copy names between them. The precedence table in planFromInput is the real behaviour and stays untouched.

It beats the tool-surface candidate on risk: no concurrency, no process boundary, no string literal. The typechecker and 137 smoke checks cover every path it changes. It beats the worker/record seam on verifiability: that seam is deeper, but its only real test is e2e, outside CI. It beats owner identity, live roster, and the pane on capability preserved: those move 5–170 lines, while this removes a whole shape from every future agent option.

CapabilityEvery spawn, resume, and send option survives; only the declaration count changes.
RiskMedium-low: pure types plus two mechanical mappings, gated by tsc and smoke.
First moveDelete WorkerSpecInput and recordFromPlan; let fire add identity to the intent and derive the spec.
